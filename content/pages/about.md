## Three generations in the same grove

Olaya began in 1962 when Rafael Olaya planted the first trees on a hillside above Cazorla. He chose the land for its altitude — 900 metres above sea level — and its orientation south-southeast, where the Sierra de Cazorla blocks the cold north wind.

His son Miguel took over in 1989. His daughter Sofía runs the estate today.

## The grove

680 trees across 14 hectares. Mostly Picual — the variety that defines this part of Jaén — with a smaller grove of Arbequina planted in 1998 and a single row of Hojiblanca on the northern edge that Rafael always said were for the wind.

The oldest trees are over a century old. We don't know exactly when they were planted — the records don't go back that far. We do know they've survived drought, frost, and three generations of family disagreements about when to harvest.

## The decision to bottle direct

For most of its history, Olaya sold in bulk to cooperatives. The oil was good — the oil has always been good — but it disappeared into blends and the estate got a commodity price for it.

In 2016, Sofía decided to stop. The harvest that year went into Olaya-labelled bottles for the first time. It sold out in six weeks. The cooperative order has not been renewed.

## Where to find us

Direct orders ship across Europe and to select markets in the UK and US. We also supply a small number of restaurants and specialist retailers — contact us if you'd like to discuss a wholesale arrangement.

The estate is not open to visitors. The grove, however, is available to walk if you happen to be passing through Cazorla and ask nicely.
